# ClevaCado presentation — speaker notes

The public interactive presentation is `/presentation`. Its ten slides put the problem and the product first, followed by the story and future. The suggested allocation is **6 minutes**, leaving about one minute for transitions and questions within the seven-minute limit. This is a planning estimate and has not been rehearsed.

1. **A clearer journey for fruit — 15 sec.** Introduce ClevaCado and the two founders, Dylan Walt and Dylan Simpson. The device is designed to help reveal handling risks experienced by fruit.
2. **Avocados bruise. Where did it happen? — 45 sec.** Bruising may only be visible after fruit has moved through a packhouse, transport, ports, distribution and retail. Drops, vibration, compression and rough handling can contribute. When damage appears later, the particular cause and location are difficult to trace. The need is better visibility and accountability, not a claim that no-one ever knows where damage happened.
3. **Quality losses reach people — 25 sec.** South Africa's avocado industry supports growers and workers and the families connected to them. The South African government reported 15,000 farm and packhouse jobs in 2023: https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/minister-thoko-didiza-signs-agreement-export-avocado-china-22-aug-2023
4. **A smart avocado in the crate — 45 sec.** The proxy is intended to travel with fruit and capture motion. The interactive visual contains the R4 outer-shell assembly STL files and locked R4 cradle STL, plus the later photo-recreated Blender electronics model. The shell and cradle are first-print/test-fit candidates; the reconstructed electronics are illustrative, with approximate component positions, and have not been fitted or validated inside them. The automatic separation and assembly are a visualization, not a demonstrated physical fit.
5. **A different event, a different signal — 45 sec.** Six individual controlled lab recordings cycle automatically: small, medium and large drop, and rubber mat, tar road and dirt road. Each 2-second crop is independently auto-scaled. The labels are source test labels, not model predictions; the recordings are not live or bruise measurements. The lab work explored classifying controlled proxy motion. The team assesses its proof of concept as TRL 4; independent fruit and supply-chain validation remains ahead.
6. **A project worth continuing — 30 sec.** At the beginning of 2026, our Investigation project offered a chance to step outside our comfort zones, think critically and use engineering on a real problem that we could continue after graduation. Walt suggested agriculture; research led us to avocados.
7. **We picked up the phone — 35 sec.** We were not growers or exporters, so we cold-called industry contacts, including Fremax Farms. Some calls led to referrals. Conversations highlighted visibility and accountability, and some people encouraged us to return once we had a product to show.
8. **From an idea to a prototype — 45 sec.** Before the Investigation, we entered EDHE, placed second at Wits and represented Wits at the regional round. Feedback and mentorship shaped our thinking. The Wits–AfreTec Challenge reinforced our belief that engineering and AI can address meaningful challenges across Africa. Over eight weeks we built sensing hardware and tested machine learning on controlled recordings. We shared the work at Wits EIE Open Day and submitted a SAUPEC paper for review; acceptance or publication is not established.
9. **Two founders, one shared purpose — 35 sec.** Walt gained early sales experience with CSX concussion software, Prevent smart mouthguards and SportGate. An earlier fintech platform was sold to Harwood Labs in early third year; that experience sparked Velzee, based at the Roux Institute. Simpson spent approximately five years buying, selling, trading and breeding exotic animals, building a base of more than 1,000 customers across Africa through product knowledge, marketing and service. Both bring that experience into ClevaCado.
10. **Next: learn in the field — 40 sec.** Improve the device; see the supply chain and collect real-world data; retrain and improve the model; work towards an initial pilot with industry contacts; return to Sir John with results. Other delicate produce and stone fruit may be future opportunities. The pilot is proposed, not completed.
